Draws a full A3 set from a room and its fixtures: floor plan, plan with wall units
and mirrors, four wall elevations, a freestanding vanity elevation, schedules and a
summary — exported as a 10-page PDF.

## Which file do I want

| | |
|---|---|
| **Bathroom-1.2.0-setup.exe** | Installs it, with a Start menu entry and a desktop shortcut. Per-user, so it needs no administrator. |
| **Bathroom-1.2.0-portable.exe** | Runs as it is, from anywhere — a USB stick, a network share. Nothing is installed. |

Windows x64. Neither is code-signed by a certificate Windows recognises, so
SmartScreen will show "Windows protected your PC" the first time — **More info →
Run anyway**.

Installing over 1.1.0 keeps your jobs. Nothing about how a job is stored has
changed, so anything drawn in 1.1.0 opens exactly as you left it.

## What's new since 1.1.0

One change, and it is the one 1.1.0 was missing.

**Height counts.** Two wall fixtures are only in each other's way if they share a
band of height as well as a stretch of wall. A toilet roll holder at 700 and a
shaving cabinet at 1420 never touch, so the roll holder hangs under the cabinet
and neither is placed with any regard to the other.

In 1.1.0 it could not. A row was a queue — a fixture's place on the wall was the
sum of everything in front of it — so two fixtures sharing a stretch of wall was
not so much disallowed as impossible to write down. A roll holder was turned away
by a cabinet six hundred millimetres above it, and on a wall with the row already
full it had nowhere left to go at all. If you have ever dragged something along a
wall and watched it refuse to pass, or jump to the far side of a fixture it could
never have touched, that was this.

It was hidden until 1.1.0 because fixtures used to shove each other along the
wall. You never found out you could not share a stretch of wall, because
everything simply barged. Stopping the barging turned a queue into a fence.

Two cabinets at the same height still keep out of each other's way, and so does
everything standing on the floor — joinery cannot be made to interpenetrate.

## Worth knowing

The dimension chain above an elevation reads end to end, so a wall row where one
fitting laps another is drawn as more than one line: the fittings standing end to
end are chained as they always were, and anything hung over them is set out from
the start of the wall on a line of its own. Every line adds up in its own right.
A wall where nothing laps draws exactly one line, unchanged.

## What's in it

- **Fixtures** — real Caroma products where the published geometry checked out
  against their stated sizes, and parametric symbols where it did not.
- **Showers** — screens and hinged doors either hand, tiled blade walls, rainheads
  on a wall arm or off the ceiling, handheld rails, twin outlets, niches, and falls
  from all four corners to the waste.
- **Tiling** — a datum with a gauge choice on the walls (full tile at the bottom,
  at the top, or split) and on the floor (full tile at either wall, or split).
- **Vanities** — per-cabinet kick height, or wall-hung with no kick; mirrors from
  10 mm deep and up to 2200 wide, set flush into the wall if you want; shaving
  cabinets with a mirror front and a recess.
- **Everything on a wall drags** into place by its grab handle, in plan or in
  elevation, to a set-out of its own. Moving one fixture moves that one and
  nothing else; anything it clears in height it simply passes.
- **SiteMeasure** for building the floor plan from measurements taken on site.
