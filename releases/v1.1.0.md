Draws a full A3 set from a room and its fixtures: floor plan, plan with wall units
and mirrors, four wall elevations, a freestanding vanity elevation, schedules and a
summary — exported as a 10-page PDF.

## Which file do I want

| | |
|---|---|
| **Bathroom-1.1.0-setup.exe** | Installs it, with a Start menu entry and a desktop shortcut. Per-user, so it needs no administrator. |
| **Bathroom-1.1.0-portable.exe** | Runs as it is, from anywhere — a USB stick, a network share. Nothing is installed. |

Windows x64. Neither is code-signed by a certificate Windows recognises, so
SmartScreen will show "Windows protected your PC" the first time — **More info →
Run anyway**.

Installing over 1.0.0 keeps your jobs. Nothing about how a job is stored has
changed, so anything drawn in 1.0.0 opens exactly as you left it.

## What's new since 1.0.0

**Every fitting has a place of its own.** Moving one moves that one. Before, a row
was laid out end to end and growing the gap in front of a fitting pushed
everything after it along the wall — drag a roll holder and the shaving cabinet
went with it. Now a fitting is put down where you drop it and everything else
stays on the number it was already standing at, however far along and however many
fittings are in between — whether that fitting's gap was dragged, typed, or came
in from an older job, and however tightly the wall is packed. Nothing is ever
pushed out of the way: a fitting dropped on top of another goes alongside it,
whichever side is nearer, and one taken well past something steps over it rather
than shoving it along the wall. Drag one past another and they change places, and
the one passed never knows. There is a **set-out figure in the fitting's own
panel** if you would rather type it than find it with the mouse, and moving a
fitting can now be undone, which it could not before.

**Low walls and blade walls.** A tiled low wall you can stand the joinery on, set
by its length and dragged by either end on the plan, tiled on its own, returned
into the elevation where it meets a corner, and drawn as wall on the plan rather
than as tiling. A blade wall stands on its own off the wall, and the shaving
cabinet hangs where you put it.

**The small accessories, as one family.** Towel rail, toilet roll holder, hand
towel rail, towel hook, soap dish and shower shelf — each at its own size, handed,
drawn from the direction it is seen, and carried on the fixtures plan.

**Toilets and taps.** The suite is drawn from its own numbers, with a seat you can
see and the lid down, and there is a back-to-wall concealed suite with the heights
that go with it. A wall mixer drawn to a published set-out, on baths and on basins,
with a height above the bench and the option of sitting on a return; the shower
mixer stays under the head.

**Basins.** A bowl shape to choose from, how it meets the bench, a bottle trap set
to a length, the tap where it actually goes, and the bowl against the wall. Move a
vanity in plan and its sink goes with it.

**Tiling.** Each wall is tiled on its own. Real lists of tiles for the walls and
the floor, a datum that can drop below finished floor level, and a niche tiled
inside with what it measures lettered in it. A niche is left open so the wall runs
through it, and takes no fillers or panels.

**Elevations.** Set-out chains stand off the drawing rather than sitting on the
wall, wall-row items are held under the ceiling instead of running off the top of
the sheet, the footer describes the wall it is under, the kick that is actually
there is the one dimensioned, and every wall is set out on the plan. The ceiling
can come down to 2100, and the active wall follows the elevation you are looking
at.

**Materials and finishes.** A real list of materials for the vanity top, with
engineered stone flagged wherever it is chosen. A palette on every colour, and the
whole materials library reachable from the finishes board.

**A user manual in the app**, covering the whole of it, on its own tab. And
**revisions**, the way the joinery app does them.

## Worth knowing on upgrade

In 1.0.0 the way to place a fitting exactly was to type a figure into **Left
filler**, and the manual said so. Those figures still hold a job exactly as it was
drawn, and they now behave the same as a fitting you dragged — a gap is a gap,
whichever way it was arrived at. **Set-out from start of run** is the field to
reach for from here.

What a filler still decides is how that gap is *drawn*: as a strip of board across
it, or as nothing. Because the gap is the figure and the board is drawn across it,
moving either fitting either side of a filler changes the gap, and the strip
drawn across it changes with it.

Group fittings that should travel together and keep their spacing. A group is the
only thing that makes one fitting move another.

## What's in it

- **Fixtures** — real Caroma products where the published geometry checked out
  against their stated sizes, and parametric symbols where it did not.
- **Showers** — screens and hinged doors either hand, tiled blade walls, rainheads
  on a wall arm or off the ceiling, handheld rails, twin outlets, niches, and falls
  from all four corners to the waste.
- **Tiling** — a datum with a gauge choice on the walls (full tile at the bottom,
  at the top, or split) and on the floor (full tile at either wall, or split).
- **Vanities** — per-cabinet kick height, or wall-hung with no kick; mirrors from
  10 mm deep and up to 2200 wide, set flush into the wall if you want; shaving
  cabinets with a mirror front and a recess.
- **Everything on a wall drags** into place by its grab handle, in plan or in
  elevation, to a set-out of its own, with a snap when it comes near a neighbour.
- **SiteMeasure** for building the floor plan from measurements taken on site.
